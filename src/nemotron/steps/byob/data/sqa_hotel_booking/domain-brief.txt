Benchmark an English hotel reservation assistant over a deterministic property-management
oracle. The assistant works the booking desk of a small chain and the oracle stands in for
the reservation system that holds its rooms, rate plans, guests and bookings.

The assistant answers which rooms a property has free for a set of dates and a party size,
what a room offers and how many people it sleeps, what a stay would cost once taxes are
added, how a rate plan handles cancellation, the loyalty tier and stored contact details of
a guest, and the current state of a booking already on file.

Three operations commit. The assistant reserves a room for a guest, cancels a booking that
is already confirmed, and replaces the stored email and phone of a guest. There is also an
operational availability sweep that is not customer facing; an incremental sweep returns at
once, while a full sweep walks every night of every room and does not come back inside an
interactive deadline.

Reserving and cancelling are asked about first. Both answer with a pending status that
quotes the total price or the cancellation penalty and change nothing until the caller
sends the same request back with confirmation. Replacing guest contact details commits
straight away and is not confirmation gated, because it holds no money and is reversible by
sending the previous values again.

A well-formed request is still refused when the room is already taken for any night of the
range, when the party is larger than the room sleeps, when check-out is not strictly after
check-in, when a stay begins in the past relative to the reservation clock, when the rate
plan does not apply to the room, when a cancellation is attempted on a booking that is not
in a confirmed state or whose free-cancellation deadline has passed, and when a contact
update supplies an address or number the system will not store.

Hotel identifiers use the HTL- prefix, rooms RM-, rate plans RATE-, guests GST- and
bookings BKG-. Every identifier is checked exactly; one the system does not hold is
reported as a structured not-found error and is never approximated to a near match. Dates
are plain calendar days in ISO 8601 year-month-day form with no time part.

The benchmark is authored and answered in English.
